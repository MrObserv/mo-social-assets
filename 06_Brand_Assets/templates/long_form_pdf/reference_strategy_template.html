Section 01 · Outcomes

01What success in this seat is

Read as outcomes rather than responsibilities, a Principal-grade enterprise observability and AIOps mandate means six things are true within three years:

  • One observability framework across infrastructure, platforms, applications, networks, cloud and third parties, with the group acting on a single version of operational truth.
  • A rationalised toolset with a defended platform decision, fewer overlapping tools, and a cost curve that bends down.
  • AIOps embedded in Major Incident Management with measurable P1 / P2 reduction and MTTD / MTTR improvement, governed so the second line and the regulator are comfortable.
  • SLI / SLO frameworks on the group's critical business services, jointly owned with engineering.
  • A high-performing team that runs this as a product, not a project.
  • Executive and risk-committee reporting that leadership can act on, and that stands up in front of auditors and the regulator.
Year one: a single operational truth and a defended platform decision. Year two: the bank stops finding out about incidents from customers. Year three: the converged platform is the regulator's favourite exhibit, not its concern.
Section 02 · Strategy

02Three years, one sentence each

Year 1 · See
One telemetry estate, one platform decision, one version of operational truth, first SLI / SLOs on critical business services, exec dashboard live.
Year 2 · Understand
AIOps through the explainability gate: correlation and noise reduction at scale, AI-assisted root cause in MIM, SOC and Service Ops on a shared telemetry pipeline.
Year 3 · Act
Bounded autonomy in production for pre-approved, reversible actions; converged operational platform; the group passes the accountability gate with the regulator comfortable.

The arc follows the Trust-Gated AIOps Maturity Model: Reactive, Correlated, Assisted, Bounded autonomy, with a data-quality, explainability and accountability gate between stages. Each gate maps to a control the regulator already requires, so progress and defensibility are the same work. Most plans promise autonomy; this one earns it gate by gate and can say exactly why in front of a risk committee.

Section 03 · Blueprint

03The enterprise observability blueprint

Three layers

  • Telemetry, own the pipes. OpenTelemetry as the strategic ingestion standard for new and migrating workloads, with a pipeline and governance tier (collection, routing, filtering, retention) so telemetry is an asset the group controls rather than per-vendor lock-in. Data residency applied at the pipeline.
  • Intelligence. The platform decision plus the AIOps engine: correlation, anomaly detection, noise reduction, assisted RCA. Governed by the gates.
  • Engagement. ServiceNow as the workflow spine: event-to-incident automation, MIM embedding, change correlation, CMDB / CSDM as the topology backbone. Executive and risk-committee reporting from one curated metrics store, not screenshots.
Section 04 · Platform decision

04Run the decision as tool rationalisation

Run it on capability patterns, not logos, and presume a two-platform target state: one observability / AIOps platform plus ServiceNow as workflow spine, with everything else scheduled for decommission.

  • Weight causal RCA and topology-aware correlation over feature checklists.
  • Score data sovereignty and deployability as hard gates given the regulatory posture.
  • Score exit cost: OTel-native ingestion reduces switching risk regardless of winner.
  • Criteria ratified before vendor conversations resume; procurement and the CISO co-own.
RFP criteria ratified and shortlist tested by day 100, decision inside month six. Consolidation savings (industry pattern around 30%) part-fund the programme.
Section 05 · Gated AIOps enablement

05AIOps enablement, gated

GateWhat you must passRegulatory expectation it maps to
G1 Data quality Unified, trustworthy telemetry; CMDB / CSDM accuracy programme; event normalisation. No ML on dirty data. Garbage in is worse with sophisticated models. Only around 46% of teams trust their own data.
G2 Explainability Every AI-flagged incident carries a "why" a commander and a validator can read. Vendor model cards on file. Regulator transparency and explainability expectations.
G3 Accountability Named human owner per automated action class, override and audit trail, independent validation, tested non-AI fallback. The gate most vendors skip and most regulators require.
G4 Track record Proven record of safe action, continuous drift monitoring, regulator comfort. Few banks pass this for major incidents. Honest position: this is aspirational, not year-three.
The regulator is not the brake on AIOps. It is the design spec. The gates that satisfy the second line are the same gates that make the automation safe to run.
Section 06 · Operating model

06Operating model and team

Central platform team running observability as a product, federated champions in application teams, SLI / SLO ownership sitting with service owners. Inherit and uplift the existing tools-and-automation capability rather than build greenfield.

  • Observability Platform lead plus engineers (telemetry pipeline, OTel adoption, platform ops, dashboards-as-code).
  • AIOps and Automation lead plus engineers (correlation, ML ops, automation workflows, ServiceNow EM integration).
  • SLI / SLO and Reporting; ITSM / CMDB integration; programme management.
  • A federated champion network: one named per critical application team.

Ramp: around 12 by day 100 (largely re-badged existing team), 20 by month 12, steady-state 22. Lean rather than padded for a multi-region banking estate.
